# 2. Design Values & Anti-Patterns

## Desired emotional character

The website should feel: authored, personal, intelligent, editorial, exploratory, technically refined, visually confident — calm where restraint is needed, expressive where impact is useful.

### Premium without being

Corporate, generic-template, SaaS-landing-page, résumé-converted-into-sections, AI-generated, over-designed agency concept, or a collection of fashionable gradients and glass panels.

### Memorable without being

Exhausting, slow, novelty-driven, purely decorative, or dependent on short-lived trends.

### Experimental without being

Inaccessible, hard to navigate, performant on desktop only, or a visual-effects demo that hides the work.

## Visual principles

- Typography carries visual identity — hierarchy, rhythm, mood, readability
- Layout feels composed, not filled — deliberate whitespace, strong alignment, controlled asymmetry
- Visual rhythm through shifts in scale, contrast, density, image treatment, pacing, background transitions
- Imagery should feel art-directed — communicate the product, not decorate the page
- Interactions need every state: default, hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, error, keyboard, touch

## Motion philosophy

Motion must have purpose: guide attention, explain relationships, create continuity, reveal information, support navigation. Avoid animation on every element, excessive parallax, scroll hijacking, motion that blocks reading, or effects that exist solely to demonstrate technical skill. All motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Storytelling

The website functions as a connected narrative. Sections create a reason to continue. Project pages explain: context, problem, stakes, constraints, user needs, business needs, decisions, alternatives, collaboration, result, lessons learned.

## Content

Content is a design material. Avoid generic designer language, unsupported claims, corporate filler, AI-generated phrasing, vague statements about passion. Writing should be specific, concise, evidence-based, human, and easy to scan.

## Explicit "do not" rules

- Do not copy reference-site layouts or visual assets
- Do not invent design details that should come from Figma
- Do not treat hover effects as complete interaction design
- Do not force every section into the same max-width container or card grid
- Do not create variety purely for novelty
- Do not use motion that interferes with comprehension
- Do not design for desktop only and ignore mobile, tablet, keyboard, or reduced-motion

## Criteria for evaluating design decisions

- Does this serve the narrative?
- Does this make the work clearer or more credible?
- Does this communicate Mr Chad's thinking, not just his output?
- Would a visitor leave with a stronger understanding of who he is and why they should hire him?
- Is this consistent with the approved Figma design?
- Does this respect accessibility, performance, and responsive quality?
- Is this intentional, not accidental?
